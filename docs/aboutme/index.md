#About Me

**Owen Switzer**

I am a mechanical engineering student at UNC Charlotte pursuing a bachelor’s degree with the intention of moving into the aerospace industry. My engineering interests are centered on mechanical design, physical systems, applied mathematics, and the process of turning theoretical concepts into functional hardware. I am particularly interested in developing the technical foundation required for work involving aerospace structures, mechanisms, propulsion-related systems, and other multidisciplinary engineering applications.

My decision to pursue engineering was influenced by both family and hands-on exposure to technical projects. My father’s career as an engineer introduced me to the profession early, while one of my closest friends helped make engineering more tangible through his work with subwoofers, motorcycles, and other mechanical and electrical projects. Seeing systems designed, modified, tested, and repaired demonstrated that engineering is not limited to calculations or theory, but depends on understanding how individual components interact within a larger system.

As I continue through mechanical engineering, I am working toward becoming an engineer who can approach technical problems systematically, evaluate multiple solutions, and justify design decisions using analysis and evidence. I want to strengthen my abilities in modeling, design, testing, manufacturing, and technical communication while building experience that can be applied directly to aerospace engineering. A major objective of my education is to develop the ability to move between analytical work and physical implementation rather than treating them as separate processes.

I intend to become an engineer capable of contributing to complex aerospace systems while remaining grounded in the mechanical principles that govern their operation. I want my work to demonstrate technical accuracy, traceable decision-making, and an ability to communicate engineering reasoning in a form that other engineers can review and build upon.

**What does it mean to defend an engineering decision — and do I currently know how to do it?**

I believe defending an engineering decision means supporting a selected solution with calculations, data, testing, physical principles, and defined design requirements rather than relying on personal preference. It also requires being able to explain what alternatives were considered, what tradeoffs were involved, and why the selected approach was the most appropriate for the problem. I currently understand this process and have applied it in coursework, but I am still developing the experience required to defend more complex engineering decisions consistently and thoroughly.
